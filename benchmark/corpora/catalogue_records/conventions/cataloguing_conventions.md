# Review conventions: returned catalogue records

Operator-authored rules for reviewing catalogue records returned before accession. Each
finding must cite the convention it rests on and at least one reference into the context
corpus.

These rules contain no quantities and rest on none. Every one is about the presence of a
field, membership of a controlled vocabulary, or agreement between two stated fields.

---

## CONV-M01 , conv-record-completeness [required]

Every record must state a title, a creator, a subject, a type and a rights term. A missing
field is an irregularity. State which field is absent from which record.

## CONV-M02 , conv-type-vocabulary [required]

The type stated for a record must be one of the permitted resource types in Section Two of the
reference corpus. A type outside that vocabulary cannot be routed to a handling rule. State
the type given and the vocabulary it is not drawn from.

## CONV-M03 , conv-rights-vocabulary [required]

The rights term stated for a record must be one of the rights terms in Section Three of the
reference corpus, and whatever that term requires must be present in the record. State the
rights term and the requirement that is not met.

## CONV-M04 , conv-restricted-source [required]

A record whose rights term is restricted or closed must state a source. A restricted record
with no source cannot be routed for permission. State the rights term and the absent field.

## CONV-M05 , conv-attribution-creator [required]

A record whose rights term is attribution must name a creator, because reuse under that term
requires credit and there is nothing to credit without one.

## CONV-M06 , conv-grounding [required]

Every finding must cite one convention and at least one reference into the context corpus. A
finding must state the record it concerns and the field or vocabulary it conflicts with. Vague
findings such as "the record looks incomplete" are not acceptable.

## CONV-M07 , conv-no-appraisal [required]

Findings record what is missing or inconsistent in the record, never a judgement about the
value, sensitivity or interest of the material described. Appraisal is the archivist's task,
not the reviewer's.
