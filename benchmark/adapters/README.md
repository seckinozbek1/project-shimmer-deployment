# Benchmark adapters

Adapters for the five external benchmarks of `docs/benchmarks/SELECTION.md`: ContraDoc, CUAD v1, SciTab, FinDVer and ContractNLI (added by the operator's decision D-S3), and for role B's synthetic tests (`synthetic/`, below). Each one turns benchmark items into test bundles in the input format `input-format-v1-draft` (`docs/INPUT_FORMAT.md`, on branch `loop/s03`), turns a run's output into labels, and scores labels against gold.

What they implement, from `docs/benchmarks/PREREGISTRATION.md`:

- section 0: opaque ids, the item table and the parts record;
- section 1: the hash split rule and the official splits, enforced at build (ContractNLI by its frozen files); the exclusion of demonstration documents; the length band of every document (`bands`);
- section 3: the official metric or the closest honest measure (each benchmark's headline named in its score), location recall, false positives (item level and unattached), refusals, and the catch layer;
- section 4: ABSTAIN and zero division in every F1-type measure;
- section 5.1 and 5.2: the output-to-label mapping (a settled answer on any unit beats a refusal; a figure-audit record is a figure-audit relation stamped `written_by: python`, one definition for all five benchmarks: a band record decides its own rule's item, Python's `missing_field`, `sum_mismatch` and `product_mismatch` records decide only in a one-rule bundle, and a model-written record always decides its rule's item), with S-1, S-2, S-3, S-5 (the earlier order, a refusal before an ok record), S-6 (every item of a semantically incomplete document ABSTAIN, D-066) and S-8 (B3 and B4 as if every record a model wrote were absent, DEV-012);
- section 5.3: the cut rule, with a per-document budget, and S-4; the exclusion of a B3 document whose run made a model call over its model's context, with S-7 (D-078, section 12 item 14);
- sections 5.5 and 5.6: the rule templates and the document text and units, with ContractNLI's merged-span fallback.

What they do not implement is listed at the end.

## Written on synthetic items, confirmed on development items

The adapters were first written from the schemas documented in `SELECTION.md` and the preregistration, with no benchmark data opened (hard limit H17), and checked on synthetic items in those schemas.

In loop stage S4c item 4 they were run on the development items role B's B-dev session passed on (development side only, opened under H17's exception for development items, decision D-032); nothing else of any release was opened. Each named constant was checked against them, every one the data confirmed is marked so in a comment (no document text is quoted), and the faults the data showed were fixed (D-038; per adapter below). The fixtures were rewritten in the release's own schema and stay hand-written synthetic items: no real item is copied into them, and no real party name or listed ticker appears in them. The checks' `development` group, reading the development items where they are present (`external/`, gitignored) and skipped where they are not, holds the counts listed in section 5.7 of the preregistration (documents and items per adapter, ContractNLI's parts and spans, ContraDoc's grouping, one-part documents and evidence sentences, Shimmer's splitter and the short evidence parts on the 61 ContractNLI agreements, FinDVer's table entries with a separator row, its CIKs and its development bands); the other development figures are read from the private run summaries the preregistration names, not held by a check.

- The items are hand-written.
- CUAD's 41 QA entries per contract are expanded by the tracked script `cuad/fixtures/make_synthetic_items.py` from hand-written contract texts and answer choices. The script reproduces the fixture byte for byte.
- FinDVer's fixture is laid out as the release is: `findver/fixtures/data/testmini.json` and the filings under `findver/fixtures/financial_reports/`.

Where a field name or a stored value could not be confirmed on development items, it stays a named constant marked PROVISIONAL, kept in one block at the top of the adapter (section 12 of the preregistration, item 5). Nothing is inferred from data at run time.

## Layout

```
benchmark/adapters/
  common/            shared code, standard library only
    ids.py           opaque ids (section 0), the hash split rule (section 1), length bands
    bundle.py        the one bundle writer: everything the input format decides lives here
    prompt.py        the one renderer of the B1 prompt (section 5.4), used by bare_model.py
                     and by the cut budget
    cut.py           the one implementation of the cut rule (section 5.3)
    records.py       the one record reader: review_data.json (B2) or the run's bus (B3, B4)
    mapping.py       run output to label (5.1, 5.2), B0, B1, S-1 to S-8 (S-4 in cli.py);
                     the over-context exclusion (5.3); the two scoring modes
                     (docs/benchmarks/SCORING_MODES.md); run-output names
    detection.py     the development scoring mode detection_dev (D-194), not the
                     preregistration's: one helper for every adapter that declares a reading
    metrics.py       section 3 measures, with ABSTAIN and zero division as section 4 says
    cli.py           the commands below
  contradoc/  cuad/  scitab/  findver/  contractnli/      adapter.py and fixtures/
  cuad_claim/        CUAD as a claim check (draft for preregistration version 3, below)
  synthetic/         B's synthetic tests: items, labels and scores (section 13), no build
  <name>/fixtures/synthetic_items.json   synthetic items in the release's schema (FinDVer:
                                         fixtures/data/testmini.json and fixtures/financial_reports/)
  <name>/fixtures/mapping_cases.json     synthetic run outputs with the expected labels
```

The checks are in `scripts/adapter_checks.py`; the verify gate runs them through `check()` (gate check 283 for the adapters; 282 runs `scripts/baseline_checks.py` for the baseline arms). `py -3.12 -X utf8 scripts/adapter_checks.py [group ...] [--external <folder>]` runs them by hand. They read only these fixtures (and, in the `development` group, the development items), work in temporary folders, and skip when `benchmark/adapters/` is absent.

## Portability

Standard library only. Nothing imports the repository's `scripts/`. Copy `benchmark/adapters/` anywhere, with `benchmark/baselines/b1_prompt.json` beside it (as `<copy>/baselines/b1_prompt.json`) or a `--b1-prompt` path, and run, for example, `py -3.12 -I <copy>/adapters/contradoc/adapter.py ...`. The `cli` check group does exactly that: it copies the tree to a temporary folder and runs, in a fresh `-I` interpreter, FinDVer, SciTab (with `--exclude`), CUAD and ContractNLI (holdout, with `--frozen-sha256` and `--parts merged`) builds, and `bands` and `split`.

## Commands

Every adapter has the same five commands:

```
py -3.12 benchmark/adapters/<name>/adapter.py build --input <benchmark file> --out <empty folder> --side development|holdout --max-context N --output-allowance N --token-counter <file.py> [--b1-prompt F] [--assignment F] [--testmini F] [--exclude F] [--reports D] [--b3-segment-tokens N] [--frozen-sha256 H] [--parts span|merged]
py -3.12 benchmark/adapters/<name>/adapter.py build --input <file> --out <folder> --development [--side holdout]     (development work only)
py -3.12 benchmark/adapters/<name>/adapter.py split --input <benchmark file> [--out <file>] [--exclude F]
py -3.12 benchmark/adapters/<name>/adapter.py bands --input <benchmark file> [--out <file>] [--exclude F] [--testmini F] [--assignment F] [--reports D] [--frozen-sha256 H]
py -3.12 benchmark/adapters/<name>/adapter.py label --items <items.json> --arm B0|B1|B2|B3|B4 [--runs <folder>] --out <labels.json> [--scoring benchmark|v2_primary|detection_dev] [--run-folder <controller run folder>]...
py -3.12 benchmark/adapters/<name>/adapter.py score --labels <labels.json> --bundles <build folder> --out <metrics.json> [--class-setting 2-class|3-class] [--paired-with <another arm's labels.json>]
```

`--reports` and `--b3-segment-tokens` are FinDVer's own options; `--frozen-sha256` and `--parts` are ContractNLI's; `--rule-declaration` is ContraDoc's and SciTab's (a declaration added to the rule heading, only as decided for the gate builds: `compare: passages` for ContraDoc, `compare: cells` for SciTab, D-471, D-474). An option an adapter does not take is refused. `score --class-setting` is the item set's declared class setting (D-380): required for SciTab, refused elsewhere. `label --run-folder` (repeatable) records the arm's selected machine type from a controller run folder, and `score --paired-with` refuses two arms on different types (D-S95 item 5).

- **build** writes one bundle per document, named by its opaque document id, plus `items.json` and `parts.json` beside the bundles. The output is byte-identical for the same input.
  - Inside each bundle: `context/<id>.md`, `context/_review_targets.json`, `conventions/rules.md` and `answer_key.json`.
  - `items.json` is the item table: `item_id`, `document_id`, `rule_id`, `benchmark`, and the source item and document ids. It holds no gold.
  - `parts.json` gives, per document:
    - the unit id and source words of every part, with its source span or context index;
    - the split key, side and cluster;
    - the word count and length band;
    - the cut record: budget, prompt overhead, words removed.
    It also gives the build's declared side, the SHA-256 of the split and testmini files it read, and the documents skipped (no text). It holds source ids and no gold; no arm's model sees it.
  - **B3 segments** (FinDVer, D-S13 item 7; `common/segments.py`). An adapter that declares `B3_SEGMENT_TOKENS` (FinDVer, 2,500) has B3 read every document in segments when the cut rule's token counter is given: the kept text, the same every arm receives, split into consecutive segments of whole parts, each segment's document file at most that many tokens (a lone part above it is a segment of its own, listed `over_limit`; a part is never split). A document of more than one segment gets a second bundle, `b3_segments/<id>/`, whose targets are the segment documents `<id>-sNN.md`, each under the document's own part headings, with the document's rules; B3 runs that bundle in place of the document's, in one run folder named by the document id. `parts.json` records per document `b3_bundle` (the bundle B3 runs), `b3_segments` (each segment's id, first and last part, tokens, `over_limit`; empty for one segment) and `b3_segment_limit`, and the build's `b3_segment_rule`. `label` reads the segments from the `parts.json` beside `items.json` and, for B3 and B4, pools every segment's records from the one run, each record's unit id translated to the document's; a call over the context in that run excludes the whole document (section 5.3). `--b3-segment-tokens N` replaces the declared limit and needs the token counter. `tools/stage_workload.py --build` stages document bundles only; a segment bundle is staged with `--bundle <build>/b3_segments/<id>`.
  - `answer_key.json` holds the input format's required fields, with empty `planted`, `clean` and `twin_of`, and the gold of every item: label, evidence units, and whether location is measurable. The repository's `.gitignore` ignores every file named `*key*`, so built bundles are never committed by accident. They are build output.
- **Sides.** Every build declares its side; a document on the other side makes the whole build refuse, writing nothing. A `--development` build that declares no side refuses a holdout document unless `--side holdout` is also given.
  - ContraDoc reads groups and sides from the frozen file written once on the whole input (`--assignment`: the adapter's own `split --out`, or the file B-dev wrote with its own implementation).
  - A FinDVer holdout build needs `--testmini` and refuses an overlapping report; with no side declared, a filing's side is its items' official split.
  - ContractNLI reads only the frozen file of its side: `dev.json` by the SHA-256 the B-dev manifest records (held in the adapter); a holdout input only under the SHA-256 role B records for `test.json` and passes with `--frozen-sha256` (the manifest records none for `test.json`, so the adapter holds none and cannot check B's value; it refuses `dev.json`'s). `split` and `bands` check the same.
- **Exclusions.** `--exclude <file>` (a JSON list of source document ids, or `{"source_document_ids": [...]}`) removes the documents a benchmark's own paper prints as in-context examples, as role B identified them, with all their items; `parts.json` records the file's SHA-256 and what was removed, and `split` marks them `excluded_demonstration`.
- **bands** gives every document's words, length band and side (as a build records it) by the one word rule over a whole input, with no build, and per band the documents, items and median words for the holdout pool alone (`holdout_pool_bands`, what role B writes S8's `B_SIZES.json` from) and for the development side apart (`development_bands`). FinDVer's `--testmini` marks a holdout filing it also holds `removed_overlap` (in no total), and a FinDVer holdout input without it is refused; ContraDoc's sides come from the frozen `--assignment` alone, and a ContraDoc input without it is refused, as a holdout build refuses it; ContractNLI's input is checked as a build checks it.
- **The cut budget** is computed per document: the context length, minus the B1 prompt rendered with an empty document from that bundle's own conventions (the largest over its items), minus the output allowance, minus 32.
  - The token counter file defines `count(text) -> int` and `count_prompt(system, user) -> int` with the main reading agent's own tokenizer and chat template.
  - A build without these inputs is refused unless it is flagged `--development`, which `parts.json` records.
- **split** gives the side of every document.
  - ContraDoc, CUAD and SciTab use the hash rule.
  - FinDVer and ContractNLI use their official splits with `--official development|holdout`. FinDVer's `--testmini <file>` marks overlapping reports.
  - `--out` writes the frozen file, with the input's SHA-256.
- **label** reads run output.
  - For B2, B3 and B4, `--runs` holds one run folder per document, named by its opaque document id.
  - For B1, `--runs` holds one `bare_model.py` output folder per document, named the same way, with `labels/<item id>.json` inside.
  - B0 needs no run.
  - Typed records are read by one reader, `common/records.py`: B2's from `review_data.json` (`findings`), B3's and B4's from the run's bus (`logs/agent_bus.jsonl`), stamped `written_by` from the line that carried them, superseded within one agent and document, a judged absence counted only by Python's stamped copy and the raw model copy listed apart (preregistration section 5.1, draft).
  - That bus reading (decision DS5-8, section 12 item 9) has been attacked by a checker and recorded (`docs/loop/DECISIONS.md` D-055 and D-056), so `mapping.BUS_READER_ADOPTED` is True and B3 and B4 are labelled from the bus; a run with no readable bus is a run failure. With the flag False they are refused (exit 4).
  - The output file holds the primary labels and the S-1, S-2, S-3, S-5, S-6, S-7 and S-8 analyses.
  - S-8 (DEV-012, preregistration section 4): B3 and B4 scored as if every record written by a model were absent. Of a document's records only those whose `written_by` is exactly `python` are kept (`mapping.python_only_view`; an unstamped record, or any other stamp, is a model's, section 3 item 6), and each item is labelled by section 5.1's lines on what remains: an item a model-written record decided takes the next record in that order (irregular, then ok, then refusal), else silence. Every other rule is unchanged: the run-failure line, D-066 (incomplete runs, `item_calls_failed`), the over-context exclusion of section 5.3, the rule-id map, the figure-audit set-aside, and the pairing-map refusal entries (Python's own map, not records). The unattached findings of S-8 are Python's alone (`unattached_s8` on each document row). For B0, B1 and B2, S-8 equals the primary analysis; the S-8 result says which (`s8_applies`, `note`), in the labels file and in the score.
  - D-066 (draft): a run that reached its end with exit code 0 and `semantic_complete` false is not a run failure; its items are labelled from its records, and S-6 makes them ABSTAIN. Each document row carries `semantic_incomplete` and `completeness` (PROCESSOR calls accepted against made, a partial delivery, the auditor pairs made from it); each item row carries `item_calls_failed`, `decided_by_partial_delivery_record` and `flagged_by_band`.
  - Registry id to operator id (section 12 item 11, adopted by D-078, `mapping.RULE_ID_MAP_ADOPTED` True): a judging call and a pairing-map refusal name the registry id (`CONV-001`); each is matched to the item's operator id through the run's own `audit/convention_assignment.json` (`by_rule`, `source_rule_id`), compared without regard to case. The item-level exception `item_calls_failed` applies through it. Where that file is missing, unreadable or not one to one (two registry ids naming one operator id), it is not used on that document: the row says why (`not_applied_no_rule_map`, `not_applied_rule_map_unreadable`, `not_applied_rule_map_not_one_to_one`), the document row carries `rule_map_status`, and `documents_rule_map_unusable` counts it.
  - Over the model's context (section 5.3, section 12 item 14, D-078): a B3 document whose run has at least one reply record under `audit/model_replies/` with `context.over_context` true (as `scripts/reply_records.py` writes it) is excluded from B3. Every item of it carries basis `over_context_excluded` (class ABSTAIN, never scored); the document row carries `over_context` (reply records read, those with a context, the calls over it, by agent) and `over_context_excluded`; the labels carry `over_context` (the excluded documents, their items, their calls by agent) and the counts `over_context_excluded_documents` and `items_over_context_excluded`. S-7 scores those items ABSTAIN (basis `s7_over_context_abstain`). An unreadable reply record is an adapter fault. B4 is not checked (`mapping.OVER_CONTEXT_ARMS`).
  - One setup-failure rule for B1 and B3 (D-084, DEV-014, `mapping.SETUP_FAILURE_ARMS`): a B1 document whose `bare_model.py` output holds `b1_setup_failure.json` (a call out of memory or on a refused kernel, or a prompt plus the output allowance over the model's context, recorded before anything is generated), and a B3 document whose run has a reply record of a call that failed out of memory or on a refused kernel (`backend_ok` false, `error_category` `out_of_memory` or `attention_kernel_refused`), had a setup failure: every item of it carries basis `setup_failure` (class ABSTAIN, never counted as an abstention), the document row carries `setup_failure` and `setup_failure_excluded`, and the labels carry `setup_failure` (documents, items) and the counts `setup_failure_documents` and `items_setup_failure`. Every analysis but S-7 leaves those documents out of that arm's scoring; S-7 scores their items ABSTAIN (basis `s7_setup_failure_abstain`); `score --paired-with` leaves out a document either arm failed on (`left_out_setup_failure`). A B3 call over the model's context keeps its own basis, `over_context_excluded`, with the same treatment.
  - D-068: a model's answer kept on a pair Python could compute nothing for is on the bus once, in Python's stamped copy (`judged_path` `uncomputable`), written by `model`; it decides its rule's item like any model-written record.
  - Two scoring modes (D-S13 item 9; the rule is `docs/benchmarks/SCORING_MODES.md`), `--scoring benchmark|v2_primary`, default `v2_primary`. `v2_primary` is everything above, unchanged. `benchmark` (the main figure) counts every model judgement the pipeline shows as advice as a finding: before section 5.1's lines, a record a model wrote that carries no `source_rule_id` takes the operator id the run's own map (`audit/convention_assignment.json`, used only when readable and one to one) gives the registry id it names (`rule_id`, else `procedure_id`, `conv_id`, `convention_ref`), as phase 6 shows it in `review_data.json`'s `model_findings_not_promoted` (`mapping.advice_view`; the record is marked `source_rule_id_from_map`). Precedence is unchanged: the lines' order, whoever wrote the record. A note on a Python record, a refused answer, a judged absence's raw copy, withheld items and a model item with no `record_verdict` decide nothing in either mode. Every analysis of a labels file and of a score carries `scoring`; in benchmark mode each B2, B3 or B4 document row carries `advice` (records, mapped, unmapped, the map's status, notes on Python's records, refused answers) and the counts add `advice_records`, `advice_mapped_by_rule_map` and `advice_unmapped`. B0, B1 and S-8 are the same in both modes. `score --paired-with` refuses two arms with records labelled under different modes. On the saved S5 development outputs the `v2_primary` labels equal the labels written before the modes existed byte for byte once `scoring` is removed.
  - A development mode, `--scoring detection_dev` (Annex D D-S26 item 1; the rule is decision D-194 in `docs/loop/DECISIONS.md`, written before any score was read with it; `common/detection.py`). It is not the preregistration's reading and changes neither mode above. It reads the same records as `v2_primary` (advice is not mapped) and the document's units from the run's own `audit/pairing_map.json`. Each unit gets the states its kept records give for the item's rule (holds: ok; violates: irregular, relation not an absence relation; lacks: irregular `missing_field` or `licence_missing`; uncertain: the UNCERTAIN marker); a unit with two states is conflicting; an answer Python refused (`absence_refused`) and a record naming no unit of the list decide nothing. Each adapter declares its reading on its `Spec` (`detection`): CUAD `exists` (present when one unit holds, absent only when every unit lacks, so one unit's absence is never the document's), ContractNLI and SciTab `three_way` (contradiction or refutes when a unit violates and none holds, entailment or supports when a unit holds and none violates, both a conflict; not mentioned or not enough info only when every unit is unsettled: lacks or uncertain; an absence relation is never a contradiction), ContraDoc `each` (contradictory when a unit violates, not contradictory only when every unit holds). Anything else is ABSTAIN. Every row carries `decided_by` (`kept_claim`, `silence`, `answer` for B1, `no_answer`, `excluded`) and `v2_primary_class`; an item with no kept claim is `silence`, ABSTAIN in every measure, its `v2_primary` label kept as `silence_label`, and `score` reports the silence rows and the labels from claims apart under `detection_dev`. B0 is all silence; B1's labels are its answers. The labels file holds `primary`, S-6, S-7 and S-8 (S-8 rows carry their own `v2_primary` class, basis and unit counts), and `score` writes no S-4; `score --paired-with` refuses a mix of this mode and another, whatever the arms. Under this mode a labels file's `counts` read its own rows and add `detection_dev`; the counts that describe the `v2_primary` decision of an item (`silent`, `refusals`, the band, partial-delivery and refusal-and-ok counts and the like) are under `v2_primary_counts`, recomputed on the rows' `v2_primary` class and basis, and the score's `items_from_refusal` is null (D-195). It is development only: a build whose parts record is missing, or names any document on a side other than `development`, is refused (exit 4). FinDVer, CUAD-claim and the synthetic suite declare no reading and are refused (exit 4). The adapter checks' `detection` group and gate check 317 hold it; the neutralisations its proofs must fail under are: silence given its `v2_primary` label (in the primary and so in S-6 and S-7), S-8 silence rows given their `v2_primary` class, S-8 rows carrying the primary row's `v2_primary` fields, CUAD absent from one unit's absence, the not-mentioned reading removed, ContraDoc not contradictory from one unit that holds, the paired-view refusal removed, the counts read as `v2_primary` again, and the development-only refusal removed.
  - The command exits 3 when it records an adapter fault (a prior relation, a missing typed-record key, an unrecognised verdict).
- **score** scores each analysis, and S-4 (the primary labels without the items `parts.json` lists as having their evidence beyond the cut). It reads gold from the bundles' `answer_key.json` and units from the bundle documents. It refuses when a labelled document has a gold item with no label. Every result carries the statement that it is not comparable with published numbers, and names its headline measure.
  - Every analysis but S-7 leaves out the documents this arm excluded for a call over the model's context; S-7 scores their items ABSTAIN. An arm that excludes nothing (B0, B1, B2, B4) is scored on all its documents.
  - S-8 is scored like every other analysis (in the paired view too); its score carries `s8_applies` and `note`, so a B0, B1 or B2 score says that S-8 equals its primary.
  - `--paired-with <labels.json>` (another arm's labels file) adds `paired`: every analysis but S-7 again, on the documents both files label and neither arm excluded, with the documents left out and why (`left_out_over_context` per arm, `left_out_not_in_both`). Comparisons of B3 with any arm read this view (section 12 item 14).

## B's synthetic tests

`synthetic/adapter.py` scores the synthetic tests role B prepares (preregistration section 13). A test is a set of bundles in the input format, each with its own `answer_key.json`; they exist already, so there is no build. It shares `common/` with the benchmark adapters.

```
py -3.12 benchmark/adapters/synthetic/adapter.py items --root <test folder> [--out items.json]
py -3.12 benchmark/adapters/synthetic/adapter.py label --items <items.json> --bundles <test folder> --arm B0|B1|B2|B3|B4 [--runs <folder>] [--run-map <map.json>] --out <labels.json> [--scoring benchmark|v2_primary]
py -3.12 benchmark/adapters/synthetic/adapter.py score --labels <labels.json> --bundles <test folder> --out <metrics.json> [--key-map <map.json>]
```

- **items**: one item per (target document, rule): each document the bundle's targets file names (`context/_review_targets.json`, or `_review_targets.json` at the top of a staged workload) and each rule heading of its conventions that carries an operator id. The answer key is never read. `items.json` carries `item_id` (the first 12 hexadecimal characters of SHA-256 of `item:synthetic:<bundle>/<document>/<rule id>`), `document_id` (the target's file stem: the id the pipeline and `bare_model.py` use), `rule_id`, `benchmark` `synthetic`, `bundle` and the source ids. It is the item table `bare_model.py` and `tools/stage_workload.py --items` take; the B1 question and labels are the `synthetic` entry of `baselines/b1_prompt.json`.
- **label**: through `common/mapping.py` (records, D-066, the over-context exclusion, the setup-failure rule, the rule-id map, S-1 to S-8). FLAG is `violated`, CLEAR `holds`; UNDECIDED is ABSTAIN. Every record decides the item of the rule it names (`Spec.attribute_as_named`; nothing is set aside, so S-3 equals the primary). A FLAG of B2, B3 or B4 carries its deciding records (`deciding_records`); a FLAG of B1 carries the units its quoted evidence lies in (`cited_unit_ids`: a quoted sentence, whitespace-normalised, a closing `.`, `!` or `?` dropped, locates the one unit whose text holds it; a sentence two units hold locates none). Units are Shimmer's (`synthetic.units`, held equal to `pairing_map.split_units`). `--runs` holds one run folder (B1: one `bare_model.py` output folder) per bundle, named by the bundle; `--run-map` names each instead.
- **score**: per arm and analysis, per bundle, per role (base, twin, probe) and in total. A planted flaw is located by B2, B3 or B4 when a record that decided a FLAG of the document, under any rule, has a unit id containing the planted unit and the planted relation (any relation when the key gives none), and its reason is confirmed when one such record states the key's claim (relation, field label when both state one, both figures): `tools/score_corpus.py`'s matching and the input format's (section 9), on deciding records only. The rule is the attribution, reported apart (`attributed`). B1 is located when its item of the planted rule is FLAG and a quoted sentence lies in the planted unit; its reason is a reviewer's (section 3.3). False positives: items flagged whose rule the key does not plant, unit-rule pairs flagged on units the key does not plant (`records_on_unplanted_units`, with how many of those records each of section 3.4's deliverables shows, `unplanted_records_per_deliverable`), and unattached records. Distractor hits: unit-rule pairs flagged on a `clean` unit. A keyed bundle's twin false alarms are the twin's flagged items and pairs when the twin is labelled in the same file. A negotiation-style key (`prior_records`) is not scored (`not_scored`). `--key-map` names a key outside the bundle.

**ContraDoc.** Confirmed on the B-dev development items (S4c item 4), none PROVISIONAL now:

- `unique id` is a string; `text` a string;
- `evidence` is one string in every `pos` record; `ref sentences` is a string, a list of strings, or absent.

`--assignment` reads the adapter's own `split --out` file and the frozen file B-dev wrote with its own implementation of section 1 (`assignments`: `{unique id: {group, side}}`), and refuses a side that is not the hash rule on its group key. **CUAD.** Confirmed on the B-dev development items (S4c item 4): the SQuAD-style container (`{version, data}`, one paragraph per contract, 41 QA entries), the `<title>__<category>` QA id, `answers` empty exactly when `is_impossible` is true, `title` as the tie-break between duplicate copies, and the data's own category spellings (`DATA_SPELLING`: 15 of the 41 differ from the paper's names, which stay in the rule text). Where the claims of one table store different cells (one development table, two of its eight claims), the document is the table as its first claim in file order stores it and `parts.json` lists the other claims under `table_variants`; claims of one table naming different papers are refused. **FinDVer.** Confirmed on the B-dev development items (S4c item 4), none PROVISIONAL now. The release keeps claims and filings apart:

- the items file (`data/testmini.json`, `data/test.json`) is a list of claims: `example_id`, `statement` (the claim), `entailment_label` (a boolean, true is entailed), `relevant_context` (integer entry ids), `subset` (`ie`, `numeric`, `knowledge`), `split` (`testmini` or `test`) and `report`, the filing's file name;
- the filing is `financial_reports/<report>`, an object whose `context` lists the entries `{id, context, type}` in id order from 0 (`CONTEXT_INDEX_BASE` 0); `type` is `paragraph` or `table`, and a table's text is pipe rows between lines of text, almost never with a separator row (3 of the 10,991 development table entries have one; held by the `development` check group).

`build --reports <folder>` names the filings' folder; by default it is `financial_reports` beside the items file's folder, as the release lays it out. A table entry is written with a separator row after the first row of each run of pipe rows, unless one is already there (section 5.6, as SciTab's table is; Shimmer reads a table's header only when a separator row follows it), and its words are its cell words and the lines of text around them. An item whose `split` names the other official split is refused. `parts.json` records per filing its SHA-256 and `issuer_cik`, the number after `/edgar/data/` in the filing's `url`, for the issuer bootstrap.

**ContractNLI.** Confirmed on the B-dev development items (S4c item 4), none PROVISIONAL now: `documents`, `id` (an integer), `text`, `spans` (character spans that segment the text, none crossing a line break), `annotation_sets` (one per agreement; the first is used), `annotations`, `choice` with the values `Entailment`, `Contradiction`, `NotMentioned`, `labels` and `hypothesis`; the hypothesis keys are `nda-1` to `nda-20` (17 keys). No development agreement's text holds a blank line, so the earlier rule (one part per blank-line paragraph) made every agreement one part. Section 5.6 now makes one part per span of the agreement's own `spans` list: on the 61 development agreements the spans are in order, never overlap and leave only whitespace between them; 63 of the 5,102 spans hold a single space and make no part, so there are 5,039 parts, 20 to 255 per agreement (median 72), 16 agreements above 99. Every evidence index names a span; one names a single-space span (on an Entailment item with five other spans). `parts.json` records the span index of every part and, per agreement, `spans` and `spans_without_text`. `--parts merged` is section 5.6's cost fallback (consecutive pieces joined into parts of at least 100 words, the last possibly shorter), used only if S5's measured cost triggers it (section 2); `parts.json` records `part_rule` and the spans each merged part holds.

## CUAD as a claim check (draft for preregistration version 3)

`cuad_claim/adapter.py` (Annex D, D-S13 item 8) builds CUAD v1 as a claim check: per contract the 41 rules `CONV-CUADC-01` to `CONV-CUADC-41`, name `Claim`, each saying that no passage of the document holds a clause of its category (the paper's name and the release's description, read at build time from `CUAD_v1_README.txt` with `--categories`, never copied here). A present category makes the claim false, refuted by a finding that quotes the clause (Python keeps a quote only when it is text of the unit); an absent one makes it true, reached by an ok record or silence with nothing to quote. FLAG is present (any irregular record that is not a refusal, Python's figure-audit records set aside as on every benchmark), CLEAR absent, UNDECIDED ABSTAIN, S-2 applies. B1 asks the preregistered question of B's synthetic tests (`violated` is present, `holds` absent; the `cuad_claim` entry of `baselines/b1_prompt.json` is that entry word for word). The benchmark name `cuad_claim` gives ids of its own. The answer spans' offsets mark the evidence units, so location is measured on present items.

No gold reaches a bundle: the 41 rules are the same text for every contract, and a build from the same input with every `answers` list emptied differs only in `answer_key.json` (the `cuadclaim` check group, which also scans every bundle for the contract's answer spans of three words or more outside its document and key, and runs the development build where `external/` holds it). A build that declares its side outside `--development` takes only the category list whose SHA-256 the B-dev manifest records. No S8 run reads this adapter until preregistration version 3 is frozen.

## Words

One rule counts words for the length band, the cut line and `parts.json` (section 5.6, Words): `common/ids.py` `word_count`, Python's `str.split()` with no argument, line by line, where on a pipe row (table text) a token made only of `-`, `:` and `|` is not a word; a SciTab cell is counted on its own as table text (`cell_word_count`). `common/cut.py` `words` is the same function, and every adapter counts its parts with it, so a table's words are its cell words wherever they are counted.
