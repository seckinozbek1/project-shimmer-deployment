## Abstract

(Written last, from sections 5 to 9 only.)

## 1. What Shimmer is

- The task: reviewing a document against an operator's rules, and following positions across negotiation rounds.
- The swarm of agents.
- The outputs a reader receives.

## 2. Why it is built this way

1. Python before the model where arithmetic decides: the paired review, RELATIONS-A, the computed findings.
2. Model output kept in view and marked, never promoted: HONEST-OUTPUT-A, WRITTEN-BY-A.
3. Governance: an append-only constitution, operator-ratified changes, and the privacy boundary (LAW-IV).
4. An autonomous loop that learns from its own attempts: bars written before reads, checkers, and the lessons record (`docs/loop/LESSONS.md`).

## 3. Benchmarks and why they fit

- For each benchmark: its task, split, size and licence; what it tests in Shimmer; and what it does not test.
- The worked cases, named as demonstrations, not as evidence.

## 4. Measurement protocol

1. Development and fresh material; exposed and sealed; role separation.
2. The preregistration and its versions.
3. The single read, and how code enforces it (the read-once key).
4. The comparability rule, stated in full, and the inventory's outcome: how many earlier results are in, how many are out, and why.
5. Statistics:
   - the tests and thresholds;
   - power stated from out-of-sample effects (LESSONS L-4);
   - how failures are worded ("not shown at this size").

## 5. Results

1. The confirmatory result, with uncertainty, against B1 and the untrained forms.
2. Comparable published systems (D-S73): every results table carries the rule's context column (section 5 of the rule), with each difference in setting stated and whether the published level was reached.
3. Earlier development results that pass the rule, each with its run, items, scorer and integrity, and its in-sample or exposed label where one applies.
